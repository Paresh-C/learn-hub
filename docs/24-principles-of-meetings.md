## 24. Principles of meetings

**Before: decide whether you need a meeting**

- Can a short document, a comment in an issue, or an async message do it? Prefer writing for status and information; use meetings for discussion, decisions, conflict, and relationship building.
- **No agenda, no meeting.** State the **purpose** and the **desired outcome** (decide X, align on Y, unblock Z).
- Invite only the people needed; mark others optional. Fewer people means better decisions.
- Send context and pre-reads ahead of time, and expect people to read them.
- Timebox: 25 or 50 minutes instead of 30 or 60 leaves buffer between meetings.

**During**

- Start and end on time. One facilitator keeps the agenda; one person takes notes.
- Everyone prepares and participates; no multitasking.
- Be concise: lead with the conclusion, then the reasoning.
- Use a **parking lot** for off-topic points so the meeting stays on track.
- Make decisions explicit: What was decided, by whom, and why. Disagree openly, then **disagree and commit**.
- End by summarizing **decisions and action items with an owner and a date.**

**After**

- Send a short written summary the same day: decisions, actions, open questions, links.
- Follow up on your actions. Record meetings for absent colleagues when allowed.

**Common meeting types**

- **Daily standup (about 15 min):** focus on the sprint goal and blockers, not a status report to a manager. Format: progress toward the goal, plan, blockers. Take long discussions offline.
- **Planning / refinement:** clarify scope and acceptance criteria, ask about edge cases and dependencies, flag risks and size.
- **Review/demo:** show working software, collect feedback.
- **Retro:** safe, blameless, focused on process; end with concrete actions.
- **Design/technical review:** share the proposal in writing first (RFC or ADR), discuss trade-offs.
- **1:1 with your manager:** your agenda first (progress, blockers, feedback, growth). Write notes and follow up.

**Participant skills**

- Ask clarifying questions; restate what you understood (Just to confirm, you mean...).
- Speak up early if something is unclear; a new hire's questions often expose missing documentation.
- Take your own notes of domain words and decisions.
- In remote or mixed settings: camera and mute etiquette, use chat for links, and write down decisions because audio is easy to forget.

**Anti-patterns:** meetings without an owner, status meetings that could be a message, too many attendees, one person talking, decisions without writing, recurring meetings that nobody reviews. Review recurring meetings regularly and cancel those that no longer serve a purpose.
